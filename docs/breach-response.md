# Breach Response Plan

## First hour
1. Contain: disable the affected account (Admin → Staff or Patients), revoke sessions, rotate keys if a secret leaked.
2. Preserve evidence: export the relevant audit log rows before anything is changed.
3. Tell the Security Officer. Start a written timeline with names, times and actions.

## First 24 hours
4. Work out what was exposed: which records, whose, and whether it was viewed or only accessible.
5. Run the four-factor risk assessment (45 CFR §164.402): the nature and extent of the information, who used or received it, whether it was actually acquired or viewed, and how far the risk has been reduced. Document each answer.
6. Decide whether it is a breach of unsecured PHI. Encrypted-at-rest data that stayed encrypted may not be.

## Notification deadlines
- **Individuals:** within 60 days of discovery, in writing, describing what happened, what was involved, what they should do, what you're doing, and how to contact you.
- **HHS Office for Civil Rights:** within 60 days if 500 or more people are affected; otherwise within 60 days of year end.
- **Media:** within 60 days if 500 or more residents of one state or jurisdiction are affected.
- **State attorneys general and boards of pharmacy:** varies, sometimes faster. Check every state where an affected patient lives.
- **Vendors:** if a business associate caused it, they must notify you without unreasonable delay, and no later than 60 days.

## Afterwards
7. Fix the cause, and record what changed.
8. Update the risk analysis.
9. Retrain anyone involved, and apply the sanction policy where appropriate.
10. Keep every document for six years.
